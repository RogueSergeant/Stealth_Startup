How It Works

Not a budget tracker. A spending blocker.

1

Load Your Prepaid Card

Impulse Card is a prepaid debit card (like Monzo or Revolut). Load money onto it and use it as your primary card for everything except pre-approved categories like groceries.

2

Set Your Rules Once

One-time setup: "Block purchases over £40 unless I justify them. Groceries unlimited. Block ASOS and Amazon after 10pm."

3

Card Blocks Automatically

Try to buy that £89 keyboard at 11pm? Declined. You get a notification: "Why do you need this right now?" Open the app to justify it.

4

AI Evaluates (Typically Under 10 Seconds)

Type your reason. AI checks it against your goals and spending patterns. Result: approved immediately, delayed 7 days for reflection, or kindly declined with a reason why. Emergency override available anytime.

Why AI? Why Not Just Simple Rules?

Because context matters more than numbers

A simple rule like "block purchases over £40" doesn't work.

Sometimes a £50 purchase is genuinely essential—medicine refills, urgent car repairs, replacing broken headphones you need for work calls tomorrow.

Sometimes a £20 purchase is pure impulse—that third pair of trainers you don't need, another book for the stack you haven't read, hobby supplies for an interest that'll fade by next week.

AI understands context, not just numbers.

It evaluates your reason against your actual spending patterns, your stated goals, and the specific purchase context. It knows the difference between "my work headphones broke and I have calls tomorrow" (legitimate) and "these would be perfect for when I start my podcast" (impulse for a project that doesn't exist yet).

Simple rules treat every £40 purchase the same. AI treats each purchase based on whether it actually makes sense for you, right now, given your patterns and goals.

It learns from how you actually spend.

If you justify three "essential" hobby purchases in two weeks then abandon them all, the AI notices. Next time you're excited about a new hobby, it'll push back harder. Not because of the amount—because of the pattern.

This isn't AI for the sake of AI. It's the only way to create intelligent friction that adapts to you.

Questions You Might Have

This sounds great, but does it actually exist yet?

No. This is a pre-launch landing page. I'm validating demand before building. If enough people want this, I'll build it. If 10 people sign up, I'll know this isn't the solution people need.

Won't this be embarrassing in public?

The decline looks exactly like any other card decline. Your notification is discreet. The approval typically takes seconds in the app. No one watching knows you're justifying a purchase. Emergency override available if genuinely urgent.

What if I just game the AI with the right words?

You might. But here's the thing: even if you learn to write justifications that always work, you're still creating friction. You're still pausing. That pause is often enough to break the impulse. If you're deliberately gaming it, you probably didn't need the card in the first place.

Can my partner/spouse see my spending?

No. This is personal accountability, not joint accountability. If you want to share access with someone, that's an optional feature we might build, but it's not the default.

Is this like parental control, but for adults?

No. You set the rules. You approve your own purchases. The AI isn't a parent - it's an accountability partner holding you to your own standards. Big difference.

What if I need to make an emergency purchase?

There'll be an instant override in the app. You can also permanently approve specific merchants. Or use the "urgent - review later" option that approves immediately but flags for review.

What if I just use my other bank card instead?

You probably will sometimes. This only works if you commit to using it as your primary card. It's not for casual use. It's for people who are desperate enough for external accountability that they're willing to lock themselves in.
