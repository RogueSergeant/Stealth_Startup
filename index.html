If This Sounds Familiar, You're Not Alone

88% of UK adults make impulse purchases. For people with ADHD, it's nearly universal.

The Late-Night Purchase

2:47am. Can't sleep. Scrolling Amazon. 'This drone will definitely make me happy.' One click. £127 gone. Morning: 'Oh. That drone.' It arrives Thursday. You open it once. £127 sitting in your cupboard.

The Hobby Cycle

New hobby idea. Mechanical keyboards. 'This is it. This is the thing.' Order £89 keycaps. £140 switches. £200 custom case. Total: £680. Monday: banking app notification. The parts are still in boxes. You've moved on to fountain pens.

The Generous Night Out

'I'll buy everyone drinks!' £100, gone. 'Don't worry, I've got this round.' Another £140. Three rounds later: £370 spent. Next morning: regret. You were just feeling good and generous in the moment.

Banks show you AFTER you overspend. Impulse Card pauses you BEFORE the purchase completes.

How It Works

Not a budget tracker. A spending decision partner.

1

Set Your Intentions Once

Choose which purchases require reflection. 'Pause me for anything over £40. Never question groceries or bills. Ask me about Amazon after 10pm.'

2

Card Pauses Automatically

Try to buy that £89 keyboard at 11pm? Purchase pauses. You get a notification: 'Worth thinking about...' Open the app to reflect on whether you need this.

3

AI Supports Your Decision

Share your thinking. AI asks reflective questions, considers your budget and goals, then supports your final decision – whether that's proceed now, wait 7 days, or skip it.

Why I'm Building This

AR
Alfie Roberts
Software Engineer • MSc Applied AI

Last year, I spent £1,623 on things I regretted within 24 hours.

£89 for a mechanical keyboard I used twice. Three Kindle books I never read. A £140 'productivity course' I never started. Hobby after hobby, each one 'definitely the thing this time.'

I have ADHD. In the moment, my brain genuinely believes every purchase is essential. By the time clarity returns, the money's gone.

I tried everything: Monzo pots, YNAB budgets, hiding cards, deleting apps. None worked because they all relied on me remembering to check them BEFORE buying. But when I see something I want, I forget those tools exist.

I needed something that didn't depend on my memory or willpower. Something that would create a mandatory pause and make me explain my thinking before completing the purchase.

So I'm building the tool I needed. Not to stop spending – to spend better.

I'm sharing this work-in-progress because I suspect I'm not alone. If you've felt that same frustration – knowing you want to spend intentionally but struggling in the moment – this is for you too.
